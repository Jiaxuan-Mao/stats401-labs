Passage preparation, coverage and reproducibility

Page numbers are one-based PDF pages, matching the printed numbering. “Introduction” and “Overview” denote an absent lower-level heading. Exact duplicate text retains its first location; therefore cross-listed courses and repeated policies are underrepresented in later sections. Counts describe retained prose, not all pages or all policy occurrences. PDF extraction is heuristic; follow the source-page link to check a passage in context.

3. Semantic embedding map

One circle is one passage. Nearby points often discuss similar ideas. UMAP coordinates have no independent semantic meaning; distance on this map is approximate.

Outline: black = selected passage; orange = its five nearest semantic neighbors. Faded points do not match all active search/filter conditions. Neighbors are calculated over the whole corpus, using original embedding cosine similarity.

Click a circle or passage button for details. Drag to pan; scroll or pinch to zoom. Topic and section filters combine with the text search. Reset view changes only the map position.

4. Topic × bulletin section matrix

Rows are formal sections; columns are the same eight semantic topics. Darker cells mean more retained passages. Counts always describe the full corpus, so they stay comparable during filtering. The table scrolls vertically and horizontally.

Hover or focus a cell for details. Clicking a cell sets both filters and clears text search; selecting a map point outlines its cell. Long row labels show the full name on hover.
