Analysis

I used the Twitter US Airline Sentiment dataset, which contains 14,640 tweets about six U.S. airlines from February 2015. I removed duplicate rows and repeated tweet IDs, leaving 14,485 unique tweets. I also checked dates and retweet counts and standardized text formatting. For TF-IDF, I used NLTK tokenization, stop-word removal, and lemmatization. I used RoBERTa to estimate sentiment from lightly normalized tweet text, without using the original sentiment labels.

I chose these mappings based on the types and characteristics of the attributes. Airline is a nominal attribute, so I use separate horizontal bars to represent the six airlines. Sentiment is categorical, so I use different colors to distinguish negative, neutral, and positive tweets. Since the number of tweets differs across airlines, I use a 100% stacked bar chart to compare sentiment proportions directly.

US Airways has the largest negative share at 64.7%, while Virgin America has the largest positive share at 38.3%. Delta has a more balanced distribution. These patterns describe the sampled tweets from February 2015, and the sentiment classifications are model estimates rather than verified judgments.
