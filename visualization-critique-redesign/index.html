Original visualization and context

The IEA Energy Sankey

The IEA Energy Sankey is an interactive visualization created by the International Energy Agency (IEA) using data from the IEA World Energy Balances. It shows how energy moves through a country or region’s energy system over time.

The diagram is organized from left to right. It begins with energy supply, including domestic production and imports. Energy then passes through transformation processes such as power and heat plants, refineries, coal transformation, gas works, and liquefaction plants. After transformation, secondary energy products such as electricity, heat, and oil products continue through the system. On the right, energy either reaches final-consumption sectors such as industry, transport, residential, commercial and public services, and agriculture, or exits the national supply through exports, international bunkers, and losses.

The tool covers more than 150 countries and regions. In general, annual data are available from 1971 to 2024, with data extending back to 1960 for OECD countries.

Intended message

The visualization shows where energy comes from, how it is transformed, where losses or exits occur, and which sectors ultimately consume it.

Audience

The intended audience includes energy analysts, policymakers, researchers, students, and informed members of the public who want to understand the structure of a country or region’s energy system.

Intended tasks

  • Trace energy flows from their sources through transformation processes to final destinations.
  • Identify dominant pathways by comparing the widths of different flows.
  • Compare transformation inputs and outputs to understand how energy is converted.
  • Inspect final consumption and exits, including sectoral use, exports, and losses.
  • Explore differences across countries, regions, and years using the interactive controls.

Strength 1 — Effective representation of the complete energy system

The Sankey structure is well suited to energy-balance data because connected ribbons show how energy moves from supply through transformation to final consumption or exits. Ribbon width represents quantity, so dominant pathways are easy to identify, while different colors distinguish energy commodities and help users follow their trajectories. This allows the overall energy system to be understood in one coordinated view.

Strength 2 — Interaction supports detailed exploration

The original visualization includes useful interactions that help users move from an overview to more detailed analysis. Users can click transformation nodes to compare input and output flows, inspect individual pathways, and explore different countries, regions, and years. Clicking a node also reveals its value and connected branches more clearly.

Weakness 1 — Difficult path tracing in dense areas

When many flows pass through the same transformation area, thin ribbons can be difficult to distinguish because nearby colors are similar and transparency is relatively high. Some transformation labels are also light and easy to overlook. Although clicking a node helps, users may still need to manually trace long or narrow pathways. Stronger filtering, highlighting, and clearer transformation labels could reduce visual clutter and improve path tracing.

Weakness 2 — Difficult quantitative comparison

Ribbon width works well for showing approximate magnitude, but similar-sized flows are harder to compare when they appear at different positions. Exact values are only shown through interaction, so comparing several flows requires repeated hovering, clicking, and remembering previous values. Direct value labels and a linked bar chart with a common baseline could make quantitative comparison more efficient.

Weakness 3 — Difficult temporal comparison

Users can select different years and keep a consistent scale, but only one year is shown at a time. When the year changes, the previous state disappears, forcing users to remember earlier widths and positions. Side-by-side comparison, change indicators, or animated playback could make temporal differences easier to identify.

Original versus redesign. The redesign improves targeted questions, temporal pattern recognition and same-page explanation. A user can start with a complete system view, focus natural gas, play through the 2020 disruption and inspect destination changes without leaving the page. It also keeps the original Sankey grammar, so the redesign remains recognizable. The trade-off is scope and detail. The public prototype uses Eurostat rather than IEA values, groups some technical items under “Other energy flows,” and limits the comparison window to 2019–2023. A persistent detail panel and stronger colors improve focus but reduce the neutral, all-at-once feel of the original. Thin flows still require “All flows” or a focused selection. These limits are deliberate: the redesign prioritizes traceability and comparison over reproducing every original category.

References: International Energy Agency, Energy Sankey; IEA, Understanding and using the Energy Balance; Eurostat, Complete energy balances, nrg_bal_c.

Redesign Rationale

Redesign Rationale

Decision 1 — Stronger filtering and highlighting

To improve path tracing, the redesign adds Major Flows and All Flows modes and strengthens the visual emphasis of selected nodes, links, and energy carriers. Colors are brighter and more saturated, while node opacity is increased for better visibility. Hovering over a flow immediately highlights the related pathway while unrelated flows fade into the background. Transformation labels are also made clearer, reducing clutter and making individual energy paths easier to follow.

Decision 2 — More direct quantitative comparison

The redesign makes values easier to access by showing flow information directly on hover. A linked detail panel on the right also presents aligned horizontal bars for sources and destinations. When a specific carrier is selected, the panel updates to show its total value and the contributions from different sources and destinations. The common baseline of the bars makes these quantities easier to compare than ribbon widths alone.

Decision 3 — Better temporal comparison

The redesign adds a 2019–2023 year slider, playback controls, a Compare with previous year option, and a same-scale-across-years setting. Users can move through the timeline continuously instead of relying on memory. When comparison is enabled, the right-side panel shows current and previous-year values for either the overall system or a selected carrier. These features make temporal increases and decreases easier to identify.
