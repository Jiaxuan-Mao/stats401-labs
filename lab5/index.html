1. Dataset Description

This dataset contains a synthetic urban transit network with 50 stations and 50 direct connections across Central, North, South, East, and West. Connections are undirected.

2. Node-Link Visualization

Drag a station to explore its connections. Hover, tap, or keyboard-focus a station or route for details. Station IDs identify the nodes; full names appear in tooltips. Positions are a simulated layout. Line length does not represent geography or travel time.

3. Node-Link Design Description

District uses categorical color, passenger volume increases symbol area, and station type uses shape. These separate location, usage, and function. Thicker routes mean longer travel times; line patterns distinguish services. Dragging separates crowded stations, highlighting traces neighbors, and tooltips give exact values.

4. Adjacency Matrix

Both axes list station IDs. Colored cells show direct connections; gray means no connection. The diagonal is marked separately. Each undirected route appears twice, symmetrically. Hover, tap, or focus a colored cell to inspect it. Small screens can scroll the matrix.

5. Matrix Design Description

Both axes share district-first ordering. Colored labels and district boundaries reveal between-district connections. Cell hue identifies service; opacity increases with travel time. Gray cells mean no connection. I leave passenger size and station shape in the node-link view to keep the matrix readable.

6. Network Questions and Findings

  1. Central stations? The node-link view is most useful, but answers only partially. Hovering highlights neighbors; comparing tooltip counts reveals 14 stations, including s7 and s23, tied at degree three, without establishing overall centrality.

  2. Strongly connected districts? The matrix is most useful and answers this well: counting colored cells within district blocks shows South–East leads with 11 links; Central–North and East–West each have nine. All districts have ten stations.

  3. Transfer / terminal topology? The node-link view works best and answers this well: diamonds mark Transfers, squares mark Terminals, and highlighted links show s23 and s7 branching, s50 as a leaf, and s43, s46, and s49 isolated.

  4. High passenger volume? The node-link view works best and answers this well: larger symbols identify s50, s49, and s48 as busiest; tooltips confirm 9,850, 9,677, and 9,504 passengers/day, respectively. The latter two are isolated.

  5. Longest direct trips? The node-link view works best and answers this well: thicker links and tooltips identify s7–s31 (16 minutes), s18–s34 (15), and s29–s50 (14) as longest. All are Express.

  6. Concentrated route types? The matrix is most useful, but answers only partially: cell colors within district blocks show services across several districts and both within-Central links as Shuttle. Sparse counts limit concentration claims.
