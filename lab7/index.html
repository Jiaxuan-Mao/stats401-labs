Temporal-network questions

  1. How does overall connectivity change across the 60 days? Connectivity fluctuates rather than increasing every day. Average daily relationships rise from 5.50 in days 1–20 to 5.60 in days 21–40 and 7.25 in days 41–60. Day 1 has 3 relationships and 6 active companies; day 60 has 9 and 12. The overview shows these fluctuations, while the network reveals that even day 60 still has separate connected groups.
  2. Which companies become more central or commercially active? Fusion Electronics (c06) becomes prominent in days 21–40: its mean daily incident value rises from $18,605.86 to $52,680.52. In days 41–60, Ion Systems (c09) and Cedar Retail (c03) lead at $54,827.50 and $45,090.51 per day. Their larger circles indicate commercial activity; tooltips show current neighbor counts. High transaction value is not the same as every definition of network centrality.
  3. When are relatively separate clusters visible? Early snapshots are fragmented. Day 1 has three disconnected pairs and six isolated companies. On day 6, c01/c02/c03/c04/c06 form one connected group and c07/c08/c09/c10/c11 form another; c05 and c12 are isolated. Pause on these days and trace the links. Stable positions aid tracking, but spatial proximity alone does not establish a connected cluster.
  4. Which relationships appear, disappear, or strengthen? Cedar Retail–Keystone Materials (c03–c11) first appears on day 41, whereas Granite Wholesale–Harbor Shipping (c07–c08) last appears on day 40. Ion Systems–Juniper Stores (c09–c10) is absent during days 21–40, then returns: its mean amount on active days increases from $12,679.95 in days 1–20 to $20,189.10 in days 41–60. Fades expose appearance/disappearance; thicker links and tooltips reveal the larger amounts.
  5. Does the network become more cross-regional? Overall, yes, although daily values vary. Cross-region relationships account for 70/110 (63.6%) of daily relationship records in days 1–20, 71/112 (63.4%) in days 21–40, and 105/145 (72.4%) in days 41–60. These are record shares, not dollar shares or shares of unique pairs across a period. Links joining differently colored nodes show cross-region activity; the daily summary provides the exact count.

For the comparisons above, the 60 days are divided into three equal 20-day periods. Mean daily company values include inactive days as zero. Relationship-strength means include only days when that pair is active.

Design and implementation

I adapted the practice's CSV/date parsing, time-based line chart, frame function, D3 interval, and Play/Pause/Reset/slider event handlers. Each frame filters transactions by day and calculates company activity with filter() and d3.sum(). A keyed D3 join updates the links. The same company objects and one force simulation persist across frames, with gentle restarts and anchors from an initial layout to preserve the mental map. Transaction rows are copied before forceLink changes endpoint IDs into node objects. The fixed overview supports comparisons that would otherwise depend on remembering earlier animation frames.
